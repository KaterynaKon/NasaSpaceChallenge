# NasaSpaceAppChallenge
 Interactive, super intelligence (SI) powered dashboard that summarizes, ranks, and interprets flames and fire behavior in microgravity to deliver fire safety insights for human space exploration.
 
# Background
Fire is a major safety risk in space exploration, as demonstrated by historical incidents such as the Apollo 1 tragedy, the Apollo 13 oxygen tank explosion, and a fire aboard the Mir space station.

In microgravity, fire behaves differently than on Earth because the absence of gravity changes airflow and eliminates natural convection. Flames can become spherical and depend mainly on oxygen diffusion.

NASA studies spacecraft fire safety through three main areas: prevention, detection, and suppression. Prevention involves understanding how materials burn under different oxygen concentrations and airflow conditions. Detection focuses on identifying smoke and fire, while suppression explores methods such as reducing oxygen levels or introducing gases like carbon dioxide and helium to extinguish flames.

# Objective
Our objective is to identify investigations most relevant to the fire safety needs of habitable crew environments.
